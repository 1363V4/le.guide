web developers do not look ok nowadays.
they are the number one users of ai, yet they can’t stop complaining about it.

reading the different blog posts,
3 main reasons emerge:

1. i can’t use my brain anymore
2. ai is stealing my joy of programming
3. the economic pressure is too strong

...one might say **they look like drug addicts**.
at least for reason 1 and 2.

"if it's hurting you so bad, just stop!".
but of course, as with addictions (i would know), it's not so simple.

this post is my humble try to find a way out
with two words: **"just type"**.

when you can't use the brain, use the hands,
and the joy of programming will come back.

---

erik satie
has this wonderfully provocative quote:

> les pianos, c'est comme les chèques,
> ça ne fait plaisir qu'à ceux qui les touchent.

an english version would be
*"pianos are like jackpots, they only please those who touch them"

it's absolutely true.
and code is like XXX,
you only enjoy it when you type it.

people say out loud on conferences: “i didn’t type a single line of code by hand in months”. me neither! and it feels like shit!

for weeks, i tried to reconnect with the joy of programming, and it was the simple joy of typing.
of hearing the sounds of the keyboard of feeling the rebounds of the keys, of trying to match the speed of my thoughts, of lagging behind yet pushing through, somehow.

code is meant to be coded.
we all have our favorite langage,
our favorite "memorized set of instructions that flows from our fingers".
that's why programmer comes from _"pro gramma"_, _“before the letter”_,
because we use words that carry more than what they are.
they're instructions, an emerging schema in our brains.

when you type "fix this" in the chat bar,
when you're waiting for claude to answer,
when you're reading in between the typing… you’re loosing that flow.
that rythm. that pleasure.

that feeling is yours.
it's yours to retake. whatever claude may say.

**replace ai psychosis with keyboard hypnosis.**

---

it doesn't have to be that antagonistic, though.

there’s none of that in claude code, sure,
at least in **how we use it now**.
but that may change.

do you know the size of the context window of the latest models (opus 5.5 and gpt luna, as i type)?
in words?

it’s a frikkin 1500-page book.
_it’s the friggin misérables_.
it’s three volumes of lord of the rings that you can type as a prompt.

llms are a way to average your thoughts:
if you ramble on for 1500 pages about how you want to build that thing, it **will** find its way through the mess!
put all of your ideas in there, pieces of code, planned developments...
no “make a todo app, no mistakes”.
no “one-shot”.
just the brain dump of a thousand ideas.

maybe that's the new paradigm.
at least it's a way out.
llms are not a tool,
not for [rich hickey](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/ai-tool-rich-hickey-kjl8e),
not for [ivan illich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tools_for_Conviviality),
because they use you instead of you using them.
but we can find a reverse uno.
i know _human intelligence_ can.

maybe in the future the product of a company will be one big ass prompt
that they run on each 31 of the month,
and they keep _that_ a secret.

---

in a lot of ways,
i'm reminded of
[this passage](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ty49GG2MnyA)
in breaking bad:
jesse's wooden box as a testament of his crafty and carefree past.

or that story of a heartbreak one day, and the dad didn’t say anything.
the dad offered to spend the weekend building, in the garage,
an wooden closet, from saw, planks and nails.
and it helped the heartbreak pass.

and that's for argument 3.
code doesn't have much value now,
except a sentimental one.

don't sell it for crack.

**just type.**
